A Little About Me

I am a systems and security engineer with 4+ years of experience in enterprise environments. Most of my work sits where Windows and Linux, identity, Microsoft 365, networking, endpoints, and security tooling meet.

I like to understand how a system is put together before changing it. Sometimes that means tracing GPOs and permissions, sometimes reviewing logs and network traffic, and sometimes opening a workstation to find that the problem is hardware after all. When a task repeats often enough, I look for a practical way to automate it with PowerShell.

I do not want to leave behind a fix that nobody else understands. I test the result, document the change, and keep a clear rollback path when the work calls for one.

How I Approach a Problem or Change

IT is a living system: if it is not measured, it is not managed.

  1. 1

    Understand It First

    What happened, who is affected, what changed recently, and which systems depend on one another.

  2. 2

    Check Before Changing

    Logs, configuration, permissions, networking, and hardware. The goal is to avoid guessing or replacing something just to see whether it helps.

  3. 3

    Make a Controlled Change

    Back up where needed, work in stages, and keep a rollback path.

  4. 4

    Verify and Leave It Clear

    Confirm the problem is actually resolved, record what changed, and document what will matter next time.
