Why Does This Matter?

🎯 What does the attacker want?

To steal a password, deliver a malicious file, access private information, or pressure someone into taking an action.

⚡ Why does it work?

Because phishing emails look credible, create urgency, and impersonate familiar services such as Microsoft, banks, management, or delivery companies.

🛡️ What stops it?

A short check before clicking: who sent it, what the domain is, whether the file was expected, and whether the request is unusual.

Five Main Signs of a Phishing Email

1. The sender looks familiar, but the domain does not

A similar-looking domain or an unnecessary addition appears instead of the familiar official site.

2. Stressful or urgent wording

“Your account will be blocked,” “Immediate approval required,” or “Click here now”—pressure is a classic attacker technique.

3. A link that does not lead to the official destination

Before clicking, check whether the link actually leads to the official website.

4. An unexpected attachment

Especially ZIP, HTML, EXE, DOCM, or a file from an unexpected source that demands unusual action.

5. An unusual request

A password reset, sharing an MFA code, an urgent payment, or entering a password again are all warning signs.

Email Examples to Review

Suspicious example

From: Account Security <alerts@secure-account-check.com>

Subject: Verify your login immediately

Dear user, We noticed suspicious access. Please verify your login now to avoid suspension.

Example that requires verification

From: Delivery Update <notice@parcel-track.info>

Subject: Package delayed - open attachment

Your delivery has been delayed. Open the attached file for details.

What Should I Do If I Already Clicked?

1. Stop immediately

Do not keep clicking, open more files, or enter any additional information.

2. Change your password

If you entered a password on a suspicious page, change it immediately and also change it on other services where you used the same password.

3. Enable multi-factor authentication

If MFA is not enabled yet, enable it now. It is not perfect, but it makes the attacker's job significantly harder.

Rule of thumb:

If something feels too urgent, too frightening, or too good to be true, stop and verify it.
