The process

From the first conversation to a live website.

A structured process keeps decisions clear, avoids surprises, and helps choose the right level of complexity.

  1. 01

    Discovery

    Understand the business, its customers, services, goals, and current digital presence.

  2. 02

    Scope

    Decide what the website genuinely needs to do—and what can be left out.

  3. 03

    Structure and design

    Build the information hierarchy, user journey, and visual direction around the brand.

  4. 04

    Development

    Use technology that fits the scope, maintenance needs, and requirements—not the other way around.

  5. 05

    Testing

    Check desktop, mobile, links, forms, performance, and core accessibility and security considerations.

  6. 06

    Launch

    Connect the domain and DNS, deploy, and validate the site in its production environment.

  7. 07

    Handover

    Explain how everything works, what needs maintenance, and what should be retained for the future.

Frequently asked questions

Before we start.

Straight answers to questions that come up in almost every project.

Have another question? Let's talk →
How long does it take to build a website?

It depends on scope, content readiness, and how quickly decisions can be made. A focused landing page is different from a multilingual service website. After a short discovery phase, I can provide a realistic estimate without promising a deadline before the work is understood.

Do I need to own a domain already?

No. Work can begin before a domain is purchased, and I can help choose, register, and configure one when needed. The domain should be registered to the business in an account the business controls.

I already have a website. Does it need to be rebuilt?

Not necessarily. First, we look at what works, what gets in the way, and the state of the underlying setup. If a focused improvement can achieve the objective, that is better than rebuilding simply for the sake of it.

Who writes the content?

The knowledge about the business comes from you. We can work with existing material, define the structure and writing guidance together, or include writing and editing in the project scope. Responsibility and cost are agreed in advance.

Will the website work well on mobile?

Yes. Mobile is part of the design and testing process, not an adjustment made at the end. Readability, navigation, forms, buttons, and content are checked across common screen sizes.

Can it connect to WhatsApp?

Yes, when that is a useful way for customers to contact you. WhatsApp, phone, email, forms, maps, and social links can be included where they serve a purpose—not added automatically.

What about Google and SEO?

The site is built with technical SEO foundations such as semantic structure, headings, metadata, performance, and internal links. That does not guarantee rankings. Ongoing SEO also involves research, content, authority, and measurement over time, and is a separate service when required.

Who owns the domain and the website?

The objective is for the business to control its business assets and accounts. There is no value in artificially locking a client to a supplier. Handover details, source code, accounts, and licenses are defined in the proposal and project scope.

Are there recurring costs?

Sometimes. A domain, hosting, CMS, or third-party service may have recurring charges. Those costs depend on the solution and are explained before implementation. Ongoing maintenance or support is not included automatically unless agreed separately.
